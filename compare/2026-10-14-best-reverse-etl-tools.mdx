## What reverse ETL is, in plain words

Let's start with the boring bit. ETL stands for extract, transform, load: you pull data from apps and databases, reshape it, and drop it into a warehouse where analysts work. Reverse ETL just runs the other way.

In Fivetran's words, reverse ETL pushes enriched, modeled data back into CRMs, marketing platforms, support desks and ad networks. dbt Labs paints the same picture: transformed data goes out to end platforms to build ad audiences, personalize email or add fields to sales records.

Per Fivetran, a typical setup has four parts.

A **source** holds the data, usually a warehouse. A **model** says what to sync, as a SQL query, a dbt model or a visual picker.

A **sync** maps fields and sets a schedule. A **destination** is the tool that receives the rows.

Most syncs run on a batch schedule, such as every hour or every day. dbt Labs adds a caution: weigh how often you hit your warehouse against what you gain, and move with care on real time. So find out what the business really needs before you chase real time.

## Do you need a reverse ETL tool at all?

Honest question, and worth asking before you open a pricing page. Fivetran's own guide names three other ways to get warehouse data into business tools, and each fits a different case.

| Option | Where it fits | Where it hurts (Fivetran's view) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Custom API code | One or two destinations and an engineer to own it | Every API change, schema change and new destination means more work |
| iPaaS tools (Zapier, Workato, Tray) | Simple app-to-app automations | Not designed to use a warehouse as the source |
| Customer data platform (CDP) | Event-based use cases | Creates a second copy of your data outside the warehouse |

One catch: Fivetran sells a reverse ETL product, so read its list with that in mind.

dbt Labs adds a caution from the modeling side: the models you sync out should not do the heavy lifting that fact and dimensional models already do, so keep them thin.

#### 4. Segment (Twilio): best for teams already on Segment

**At a glance**
- Billing: Records successfully sent to each destination
- Free option: a free trial, per Segment's pricing page; length not stated
- Price: No dollar figure readable for Reverse ETL; CDP plans say contact sales

Twilio's setup docs say Segment Reverse ETL pulls rows with a query you provide, and the source setup guides cover Azure, BigQuery, Databricks, Db2, Postgres, Redshift and Snowflake. Models can be SQL or dbt.

**What's good**

- The limits are printed in the [system reference](https://www.twilio.com/docs/segment/connections/reverse-etl/system). A sync handles up to 150 million records, and extra rows roll into the next scheduled or manual sync.
- The same page says Segment does not limit how many Reverse ETL records you can process in one monthly billing cycle. Records that are processed but not delivered do not count toward your record limits.
- It computes changes inside your warehouse and uses the unique identifier column to detect new, updated and deleted records.
- The setup guide says BigQuery, Databricks, Postgres, Redshift and Snowflake sources can use Segment's dbt extension for model management, versioning and CI checks. A source can have multiple models.
- Per Segment's FAQ, you can switch on alerts for failed or partially successful syncs, and Segment sends a notification for every sync failure.

**Where it falls short**

- The shortest gap between syncs is 15 minutes.
- The setup guide warns that you need a user with both read and write access to the warehouse. Segment keeps its state tables in a dedicated schema, for example `_segment_reverse_etl`.
- The system reference warns that there may be cost implications to having Segment query your warehouse tables.
- Usage counts each destination separately: 50K records to Braze plus 50K to Mixpanel is 100K.
- Per the FAQ, functions are not supported, and the query builder takes one query.
- A sync is capped at 512 columns, 512 KiB per record and 128 KiB per column value (system reference).

## Hidden costs and limits to check before you buy

The sticker price is only half the story. Three checks save the most pain.

**Where does sync state live?** Tools that track what changed need somewhere to keep the record. Segment and RudderStack create tables in your warehouse. Fivetran's Advanced engine keeps state in your warehouse too, and its Basic engine keeps it on Fivetran's side.

In-warehouse state means write access and your own compute, so ask your security team first. (They'll thank you for asking early.)

![Diagram of sync state kept on the vendor's side with read-only access, or inside your warehouse with write access](/img/blog/2026/10/best-reverse-etl-tools-sync-state.webp)

**What is counted?** Active syncs, changed rows, records per destination and events are four different meters.

The worked example below shows how one job reads on each meter. Do the sums for your own numbers before you commit.

**How fast is fast enough?** Published floors range from hourly (Hightouch Free and Self-serve) to 15 minutes (Segment). Hevo Activate says 5 minutes, but it is in early access. dbt Labs says to weigh how often you hit your warehouse against the benefit and to move with caution on real time, so ask what the business needs before you pay for speed.

![Diagram matching teams to reverse ETL options, from vendor-run tools to open source and custom code](/img/blog/2026/10/best-reverse-etl-tools-which-fits.webp)

### A worked example: same data, four meters

Picture one table of 50,000 changed customer rows that you send to two tools, Braze and Mixpanel. We use the vendors' own counting rules and the prices the vendors print.

| Tool | What is counted | What the bill sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Segment | Records sent to each destination | 100,000 records (50K to each tool) |
| RudderStack | Events; each record to each destination is charged separately | 100,000 events by our reading, against a Free cap of 250K a month |
| Fivetran Activations | Monthly Active Rows for inserted and updated data | Up to 50,000 active rows a month, above the 3,500 free cap. On Standard list rates that is about $259.14 if it counts once ($199.25 base from 10,000 MAR plus 0.04 x $1,497.16), or about $518.27 if each destination counts separately. Our arithmetic on list prices from the Service Consumption Table; the docs count rows separately for each activation, and the Support Policy defines an activation as one source to one destination, so we read the higher figure as the likelier one |
| Hightouch | Active syncs | 2 syncs, which fits the free plan, though the free plan runs hourly at most |

Sources: Twilio Segment system reference, RudderStack FAQ, RudderStack pricing, Hightouch pricing docs. Fivetran figures are our arithmetic on the Service Consumption Table above.

Here's the thing: the point is not which row is cheapest, because Segment and Hightouch print no per-unit price on the pages we read, while Fivetran prints its rates in a separate table. The point is that the same job looks very different on each meter. So ask each vendor to price your own row counts.

### Questions to ask before you sign

1. Which meter do you bill on, and what is my monthly count with my own tables?
2. Where is sync state stored, and does the tool need write access to my warehouse?
3. What is the shortest sync gap on the plan I would buy?
4. What happens to a failed sync: is it retried, replayed or lost?
5. Which of my destinations are supported natively, and which need custom code?
6. What does the vendor cost at 10 times my volume?

That fills a lake that engines such as Athena, Spark, Flink and Trino can read ([compatibility](/docs/understanding/compatibility-engines/)). It does not send data on to CRMs or ad tools.

## Conclusion

Reverse ETL is a simple idea: take the tables your team already trusts and put them where people work. Picking a tool is the hard part. The meters, the sync floors and the access each one needs all differ, and several prices aren't public.

So start from your own situation.

Already on Fivetran or Segment? Test their own product first. Want a free start?

Try Hightouch or RudderStack, and if you'd rather host the software yourself, look at Multiwoven. Whatever you pick, ask where sync state is kept before anyone signs.
