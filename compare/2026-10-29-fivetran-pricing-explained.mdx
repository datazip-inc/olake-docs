## How Fivetran pricing works

Let's start with the unit, because everything else hangs on it. Fivetran's usage-based pricing docs say usage is "measured in Monthly Active Rows (MAR)". Each MAR is a distinct row, tracked by its primary key.

A primary key is just the unique ID of a row in a table. If a table has none, Fivetran makes up a hashed one, and most connections sync only new, changed or deleted rows.

Keys are counted separately for each account, destination, connection, table, activation and activation sync. Once a row is active, it counts only once that month. Fivetran's own example says it best: a row synced 30 times in a month is still one MAR.

So what does Fivetran actually bill? Three things:

- **Connections** move data in. They are measured in MAR.
- **Activations** move data from your warehouse out to other tools. They are also measured in MAR.
- **Transformations** run models on the data you have loaded. They are measured in successful model runs, with up to 5,000 free each month ([transformations docs](https://fivetran.com/docs/transformations)) (checked Oct 2026).

### What counts as a row, with Fivetran's own example

Here's the example from Fivetran's docs, because it's the clearest one around. A table has three rows, and row c has a counter of 3.

Update it to 4, then to 5, in the same month, and you still have 1 active row. Touch row a too, and the table has 2.

Fair warning, from Fivetran's usage-based pricing docs: if your source changes years-old records on every sync, Fivetran may re-import the whole table. A large share of it can then count as active.

![Two lanes: free rows like initial syncs and re-syncs, and paid rows like inserts, updates and deletes](/img/blog/2026/10/fivetran-pricing-explained-free-vs-paid-mar.webp)

Some rows are synced but not billed. The usage-based pricing docs list these as free MAR:

- initial syncs
- connection trials
- re-syncs
- Fivetran's own system tables
- the Free plan and promotions

Everything else that changed in the source since the last sync is paid, or incremental, MAR. That includes re-imported rows that changed.

### A worked example: one connection, one month

Let's make this concrete. Our example below is built from Fivetran's counting rules, and it shows MAR only. The next section turns MAR into dollars.

| Row (primary key) | What happens this month | MAR on connection A |
|---|---|---|
| 101 | Inserted on the 2nd, updated on the 9th and the 20th | 1 |
| 102 | Updated 30 times | 1 |
| 103 | Inserted on the 4th, deleted on the 15th | 1 |
| 104 | Deleted, with no other change this month | 1 |
| 105 | Not touched | 0 |
| **Total** | | **4** |

Now send the same source to a second destination through connection B. Fivetran counts connections separately, even when they sync the same data. Connection B has its own total of 4, so the same source data adds up to 8 MAR.

### A production-database example, with made-up numbers

The four sample connectors Fivetran shows are marketing and analytics tools, and the biggest has under 850,000 MAR. Your production database probably looks different. So here's a bigger case with hypothetical row counts, to show which quantities Fivetran bills and which it doesn't.

Dollar figures come from the rate card in the next section.

Picture an orders table with 10 million rows.

| Measure | Number | Billed? |
|---|---|---|
| Rows in the table | 10,000,000 | No |
| Initial load of those rows | 10,000,000 | No, initial syncs are free MAR |
| Change events this month | 450,000 | No |
| Distinct rows touched this month | 109,000 | Yes, as MAR |
| Same data sent to a second destination | 109,000 more | Yes, 218,000 in total |

Where does 109,000 come from? Like this:

- 40,000 new rows were inserted.
- 90,000 rows were updated, but 25,000 of those were the new rows. That adds 65,000 more.
- 5,000 rows were deleted, and 1,000 of them were also updated that month. The other 4,000 were deleted and nothing else, so they add 4,000 more.

None of those 4,000 deleted-only rows were inserted or updated that month. So the total is 40,000 + 65,000 + 4,000 = 109,000.

Three different numbers are in play: table size, change events and distinct rows touched. Fivetran bills the third one. So a busy table with few distinct rows can be cheap, while a small table that touches lots of different rows can be pricey.

## The Rate Card: What a Connection Costs

The list rates sit in the Service Consumption Table, version 10, effective February 1, 2026 (checked Oct 2026). That page is linked from the pricing page, but the numbers aren't on the pricing page itself. Every dollar value below is a list price, before any annual-contract discount.

Each row of the table is a volume threshold. The formula Fivetran prints is: base spend per month, plus (your MAR minus the threshold) times the incremental spend per million MAR. Partial millions are prorated, so you don't pay for a whole million you didn't use.

Here are the Standard plan rows, in plain words:

| Connection MAR in a month | Base spend | Rate for each extra million MAR |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 1 million | $5 | $495 |
| 1 million to 4 million | $500 | $170 |
| 4 million to 16 million | $1,010 | $58 |
| 16 million to 64 million | $1,706 | $19.70 |
| 64 million to 250 million | $2,651.60 | $6.72 |
| 250 million to 1 billion | $3,901.52 | $2.29 |
| Above 1 billion | $5,619.02 | $1.18 |

Enterprise and Business Critical use the same shape with higher rates. Their first-tier rate is $661.67 and $1,061.67 per million MAR (checked Oct 2026), about 34% more and 2.1 times the Standard rate by our arithmetic.

### Worked examples at list price

Time to do some sums. These use the Standard plan and the formula above, and the arithmetic is ours.

| One connection, one month | Calculation | List price |
|---|---|---|
| 21,993 MAR (Fivetran's Google Analytics 4 sample) | $5 + 0.021993 x $495 | about $15.89 |
| 100,000 MAR | $5 + 0.1 x $495 | $54.50 |
| 500,000 MAR | $5 + 0.5 x $495 | $252.50 |
| 1 million MAR | $5 + 1 x $495 | $500 |
| 5 million MAR | $1,010 + 1 x $58 | $1,068 |
| 20 million MAR | $1,706 + 4 x $19.70 | $1,784.80 |

See how fast the rate falls? One million MAR costs $500, which is $500 per million. Twenty million costs $1,784.80, about $89 per million.

Back to the production-database example, with 109,000 MAR on one connection:

| Plan | Calculation | List price per month |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | $5 + 0.109 x $495 | about $58.95 |
| Enterprise | $5 + 0.109 x $661.67 | about $77.12 |
| Business Critical | $5 + 0.109 x $1,061.67 | about $120.72 |

Remember the second destination? That's a second connection, so on Standard the bill doubles to about $118.

### Each connection has its own curve

This is the part people miss. The Service Consumption Table says each connection's MAR goes on its own curve, and you can't combine MAR across connections, activations or accounts for a better rate (checked Oct 2026).

Connection count becomes a cost lever. Four million MAR on one Standard connection costs $1,010 a month at list price. Split across four connections with 1 million MAR each, it costs 4 x $500, or $2,000.

![Four million rows on one Fivetran connection cost $1,010 a month; the same rows split across four connections cost $2,000](/img/blog/2026/10/fivetran-pricing-explained-split-connections.webp)

*Our arithmetic from the Service Consumption Table (checked Oct 2026).*

Whether you get to pick one big connection depends on the source. If you do, it's cheaper per row.

### Does the $5 get added to the row charge?

Yes, on the rate card. The $5 is the base spend in the first row, and the formula adds the row charge on top of it. Fivetran's four sample prices on the pricing page match that formula to within a cent (checked Oct 2026), and that wouldn't work if $5 were only a floor.

The billing page calls it a "minimum charge", while the 2026 pricing updates page uses both "minimum charge" and "base charge" (checked Oct 2026). Our read: the rate card is the clearest source, and your order form is the one that actually binds you.

## The four plans

The pricing page lists four plans. It puts $0 on the Free plan and prints no per-row price for the other three (checked Oct 2026). Those rates live in the Service Consumption Table.

| Plan | What the page says is included | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Standard features, free up to 500,000 MAR for connections, 3,500 MAR for activations and 5,000 model runs a month | $0 |
| Standard | Unlimited users, 15-minute syncs, 700+ managed connectors, 200+ activation destinations, dbt Core integration, REST API | $5 base, then $495 per million MAR (list) |
| Enterprise | Everything in Standard, plus 1-minute syncs, enterprise database connectors, custom roles, SCIM user provisioning, hybrid deployment, a choice of cloud | $5 base, then $661.67 per million MAR (list) |
| Business Critical | Everything in Enterprise, plus customer-managed encryption keys, PCI DSS Level 1 certification and private networking | $5 base, then $1,061.67 per million MAR (list) |

A few terms in that table need plain words:

- **SCIM** adds and removes users automatically from your company's login system.
- **Hybrid deployment** keeps the data moving inside your own network.
- **PCI DSS Level 1** is the highest level of a card-payment security standard.

One more rule from the billing and plans docs, and it's a sneaky one: only the Enterprise and Business Critical plans offer enterprise database connectors (checked Oct 2026). It lists several Oracle sources, two sources from SAP (a business-software vendor), and Db2 for z/OS and Db2 for LUW (both in Beta). High-Volume Agent connectors for Oracle and SQL Server are on the list too.

So if your main job is copying one of those databases, the connector picks your plan for you. And the plan sets the price.

## Annual Contracts and Pay-as-You-Go

According to its billing and plans docs, you can buy Fivetran two ways (checked Oct 2026).

![Annual contracts start at $12,000 with a discount; pay-as-you-go has no minimum, list price and a $5 small-connection charge](/img/blog/2026/10/fivetran-pricing-explained-annual-vs-payg.webp)

| | Annual contract | Monthly pay-as-you-go |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum | $12,000 a year | No MAR minimum |
| Discount | 5% built in, and more on larger contracts | List price, no built-in discount |
| Billing | Contracted spend bought up front | Billed monthly, after use |
| Cancel | Fixed term | Any time |
| Small connections | The $5 charge starts at renewal | $5 per connection with 1 to 1 million MAR, from January 1, 2026. The billing page calls it a minimum charge |

Bigger contract, bigger discount. On the Standard plan, the billing and plans docs show 5% for contracts up to $37,900 at list price (checked Oct 2026).

Between $37,900 and $145,340 the discount runs from 5% to 22.6%, and it is 22.6% or more above that. Business Critical goes further, to 36.42%. The pricing page rounds the top end to "save up to 22%" (checked Oct 2026).

The usage-based pricing docs list 14 days of free usage for every new connection, and the billing and plans docs list a 14-day trial for every new account (checked Oct 2026).

### Does an annual contract pay off?

Don't let the percentage discount fool you. The $12,000 minimum is the real hurdle.

Fivetran's own sample profile costs $511.15 a month at list price (checked Oct 2026), which is $6,133.80 over a year. That's well below the $12,000 minimum.

Here's another way to see it: $12,000 a year is $1,000 a month.

Picture one Standard connection: list price reaches $1,000 at about 3.9 million MAR ($500 + 2.94 x $170). Spread across several connections, you need more total rows to get there, because each connection starts its own curve.

So an annual contract suits a team with large or fast-growing volume. There's a safety net, too: the Service Consumption Table lets you carry over 100% of unused contracted spend into a qualifying new purchase (checked Oct 2026), which lowers the risk of overbuying. The break-even figures above are our arithmetic.

## Pricing Changes You Should Know About

Fivetran launched updated usage-based pricing on March 1, 2025. Then in 2026 it added the $5 charge and started counting deletes, per its 2026 pricing updates (checked Oct 2026). Which date hits you depends on how you pay.

| How you pay | When the $5 charge and the delete rule apply |
|---|---|
| Pay-as-you-go | From January 1, 2026 |
| Annual contract signed after March 1, 2025 | At renewal |
| Annual contract signed before March 1, 2025 | Moving to the March 2025 pricing happens at renewal. For deletes, Fivetran says the rules "already apply" under its pre-March 2025 pricing, so you see no change. When the $5 charge starts for these contracts is not stated on the pages we read |

## The costs that surprise people

### A $5 charge on small connections

A $5 charge applies to connections that generate between 1 and 1 million paid MAR a month. It does not apply to connections with no paid MAR, to inactive connections, or to the Free plan (checked Oct 2026).

The update page calls it a base charge, and the billing page calls it a minimum charge. As shown in the rate card section, the rate table adds it to the row charge. So a small connection whose rows come to $3 pays $8.

It's small money until it isn't. Add ten small connections, each syncing a few thousand rows a month, to Fivetran's four examples, and the base adds at least $50 a month. Thirty small connections add at least $150.

### Deletes now count

Under the March 2025 pricing, deleted rows did not count toward paid MAR until the 2026 change. Now inserts, updates and deletes all do (checked Oct 2026). The dates are in the table above, and some older annual contracts already counted deletes.

A row counts once a month, so a delete adds to your bill only if that row hasn't already counted that month.

Row 104 in the worked example is the one that adds MAR: it was deleted and nothing else happened to it. Why does Fivetran count it? In its words: "deletes in the source are changes in data that have analytical value" (checked Oct 2026).

### History mode counts every change

History mode keeps old versions of a row in your destination, which is handy. But the 2026 pricing updates say that, as of January 1, 2026, the changes tracked in history mode count toward paid MAR (checked Oct 2026).

In its words, whenever a record's value changes in a history mode table, "we insert a new row into the destination table" (checked Oct 2026). That new row counts, so every change is a bill line.

### Transformations are billed on their own meter

Transformations run on a separate meter. They bill by model runs, not rows, and the first 5,000 runs a month are free. After that it's $0.01 per run to 30,000, $0.007 per run to 100,000 and $0.002 per run above that (checked Oct 2026).

## How to lower the bill, using Fivetran's own rules

These levers come from Fivetran's own pages, linked on each line.

- **Use the Free plan for small sources.** It covers up to 500,000 MAR for connections with no base charge (checked Oct 2026).
- **Watch what your source rewrites.** If a job updates years-old records on every run, Fivetran may re-import a large part of the table.
- **Block columns you do not need.** The usage-based pricing docs say updates to a blocked column that is not a primary key do not count toward MAR.
- **Turn off history mode where you do not need it.** Its tracked changes now count as paid rows.
- **Watch the Usage tab.** Your Fivetran dashboard tracks usage, so you can spot the biggest meter first.
- **Use the estimator before you commit.** Fivetran offers a pricing estimator on its pricing page. Run your real connector list through it.

## Before you ask for a quote

To avoid surprises in a quote, do the homework first. List your connections, price each one with the rate card, and mark the small ones and the tables with deletes or history mode.

### Ask Fivetran these

- Does my order form use the published Service Consumption Table rates, or different ones?
- Is the $5 per connection added to the row charge on my order form?
- Will the estimator result for my connector list be written into the quote?
- How does the discount change as my contract grows past $37,900 at list price?
- What counts as free on my account: re-syncs, trials, promotions?

## Which option fits which team

Choose Fivetran if you want a hosted service, lots of ready-made connectors, or enterprise database connectors such as its Oracle connectors, which its pricing page lists on the Enterprise plan (checked Oct 2026). Its best case is less work for your team, in return for a bill that depends on usage you can't fully predict.

Self-hosting open-source ingestion suits a team that mostly lands production databases in a lake or warehouse and can run the software. There's no per-row bill, but that doesn't make it cheaper. Four cost inputs take its place: compute, storage requests, table maintenance and operator time.

OLake Go is our open-source option here. It copies PostgreSQL, MySQL and MariaDB, MongoDB and SQL Server into Apache Iceberg or Parquet and has no per-row bill (see [our docs](/docs/)). Table maintenance comes from OLake Fusion (see the [compaction types](/docs/fusion/compaction/types-of-compaction/)).

Fivetran's docs say its [Managed Data Lake Service](https://fivetran.com/docs/destinations/managed-data-lake-service) keeps data in your own cloud storage as managed data lake tables (checked Oct 2026). So the real question is cost, and who runs it.

To compare fairly, add four monthly numbers for self-hosting: ingestion compute, storage requests, table maintenance compute and operator hours at your hourly cost. Put that total next to the rate-card price for the same connections. Connection count alone won't tell you which is cheaper.

## Conclusion

So, what will you pay?

Price comes down to how many distinct rows change, how many connections you run, and which plan your connectors force on you. The rate card is public, which is good news, because you can estimate it yourself before you ever talk to sales. If your data is mostly databases going into a lake, price OLake Go beside it: no per-row bill, with the cost moved to your own servers and staff. Before you sign, do three things:

1. List your connections and mark the small ones and the ones with deletes.
2. Price each one with the Service Consumption Table, then run the list through Fivetran's estimator.
3. Get your order-form rates and contract dates in writing.
