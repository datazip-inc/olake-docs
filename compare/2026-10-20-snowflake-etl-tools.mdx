<TLDR title='Key takeaways'>

The best ETL (extract, transform, load) tool for Snowflake depends on where your data starts. Every load sends you two bills, one from the tool vendor and one from Snowflake for the compute the load uses. If your data already lands as files or rows, Snowflake's own loading features (Snowpipe, Snowpipe Streaming) add no second vendor fee, so price them first.

For lots of SaaS apps, look at Fivetran, Hevo or Airbyte.

For live database changes, look at Estuary, Fivetran or Snowflake's Openflow. For open-source database replication into Iceberg tables in Snowflake, look at OLake Go.

For the transform step, think dbt, Matillion or dynamic tables.

- **Two bills.** The tool vendor bills by rows, events, credits or gigabytes. Then Snowflake bills you separately for the warehouse time or loading service the data uses.
- **Freshness costs real money on the Snowflake side.** In our worked example below, syncing every 5 minutes uses 12 times the warehouse credits of syncing every hour. Same tool, same data.
- **Snowflake already loads data.** `COPY INTO` (a command that loads files from cloud storage), Snowpipe, Snowpipe Streaming, a Kafka connector and Openflow are all built in, so a third-party tool is optional.
- **Transform tools don't load data.** dbt and Snowflake dynamic tables transform data that is already in Snowflake.
- **For Iceberg, check early.** Several options below document Iceberg tables that Snowflake reads or hosts, and some are Beta, Preview or Early Access. OLake Go (v0.11.0+) creates them in Snowflake through the Horizon Catalog.

</TLDR>

Every price below comes from the vendor's own page, checked in October 2026. Plans change often, so double-check before you decide.

Where a vendor does not publish a price, we say "not published".

## ETL, ELT and Snowflake, in plain words

ETL means extract, transform, load: copy data out of a source, reshape it, then store it. ELT (extract, load, transform) flips the last two steps, so the raw data lands first and gets reshaped inside the warehouse with SQL (the standard database query language).

Snowflake can run that reshaping itself, so ELT is a common fit. Most tools below are loaders (the "E" and "L"), and a smaller group does the "T" inside Snowflake. A full pipeline is usually one tool from each group, or one tool that does both.

For a longer walk-through, see our [ETL pipelines guide](/learn/etl-pipelines/).

## The two bills (and why they matter)

![Loading into Snowflake creates two bills: the tool vendor's fee and Snowflake credits for warehouse or loading compute](/img/blog/2026/10/snowflake-etl-tools-bill-meters.webp)

Snowflake sells compute as credits.

In Snowflake's [Service Consumption Table](https://www.snowflake.com/legal-files/CreditConsumptionTable.pdf) (checked Oct 2026), an `X-Small` warehouse uses 1 credit per hour. One credit costs $2.00 on the Standard edition in AWS US East (Northern Virginia). Other editions and regions cost more. Enterprise is $3.00 in the same region, and AWS EU Frankfurt is $2.60 on Standard.

A loader that runs often keeps a warehouse awake, and Snowflake bills you for that time, not the tool. So a tool with a cheap sticker price can still add up to a big Snowflake bill.

## Worked example: what a faster sync does to your Snowflake bill

This example uses only the documented rates above, plus one assumption of ours: each sync keeps an `X-Small` warehouse awake for 3 minutes, including the minute before it suspends.

[Fivetran's Snowflake documentation](https://fivetran.com/docs/destinations/snowflake) says Snowflake bills compute per second, and recommends an `X-Small` warehouse with one-minute auto-suspend. Our 3-minute figure is an assumption for illustration.

| Sync every | Syncs a day | Warehouse minutes a day | Credits a day | Cost a day at $2.00 | Cost over 30 days |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 60 minutes | 24 | 72 | 1.2 | $2.40 | $72 |
| 15 minutes | 96 | 288 | 4.8 | $9.60 | $288 |
| 5 minutes | 288 | 864 | 14.4 | $28.80 | $864 |

Sources: the $2.00 and $3.00 credit prices are from the [Snowflake Service Consumption Table](https://www.snowflake.com/legal-files/CreditConsumptionTable.pdf) (checked Oct 2026). The sync counts and minutes are our own assumptions.

The tool fee stayed put, and only the Snowflake side changed, by a factor of 12 between hourly and every 5 minutes. On Enterprise at $3.00 a credit, multiply every cost by 1.5.

Ask how fresh your data needs to be. A dashboard that someone reads once a morning doesn't need a 5-minute sync.

## The 10 best ETL tools for Snowflake

### Built into Snowflake

#### 1. Snowflake loading (`COPY INTO`, Snowpipe, Snowpipe Streaming): for data that already lands as files or rows

**At a glance**
- Billed by: Snowflake credits. Snowpipe is 0.0037 credits per GB, with text files such as CSV and JSON counted at uncompressed size, per the [Snowflake Service Consumption Table](https://www.snowflake.com/legal-files/CreditConsumptionTable.pdf) (checked Oct 2026)
- Price: Snowpipe Streaming (high-performance architecture) is also 0.0037 credits per uncompressed GB, so about $0.0074 per GB at $2.00 a credit (same [table](https://www.snowflake.com/legal-files/CreditConsumptionTable.pdf) (checked Oct 2026))
- Status: active, part of Snowflake

[Snowflake's data loading overview](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-load-overview) lists four ways in. `COPY INTO` (a command that loads files already in cloud storage) handles batches. Snowpipe loads small batches and makes them available within minutes after files reach a stage.

Snowpipe Streaming writes rows straight to tables without staged files. A Kafka connector reads topics from an Apache Kafka server.

**What's good**

- Snowflake says Snowpipe Streaming is designed for ingest speeds of up to 20 GB/s per table, with ingest-to-queryable latency as low as 5 seconds. It adds that results depend on workload shape and configuration ([Snowpipe Streaming overview](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-load-snowpipe-streaming-overview)).
- The same page gives a plain rule for picking a path. Use Snowpipe Streaming when data arrives as rows from applications, devices or services and you need low latency. Use Snowpipe when your pipeline already produces files in cloud storage and batch-style loading is fine.
- Snowpipe Streaming can stream rows into Snowflake tables or into Snowflake-managed Apache Iceberg tables, so the Iceberg column in the table above needs no second vendor here.
- Snowflake's [Service Consumption Table](https://www.snowflake.com/legal-files/CreditConsumptionTable.pdf) says binary files such as Parquet, Avro and ORC are charged for their observed size whatever the compression. Classic Snowpipe Streaming is 0.01 credits per client instance per hour (checked Oct 2026).
- Warehouse time is billed per second after a 60-second minimum, and a suspended warehouse does not use any credits ([Snowflake compute cost docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/cost-understanding-compute)) (checked Oct 2026). That is the rule behind the worked example above.

**Choose it if** your applications or an upstream tool already produce files in cloud storage or rows in a stream, and you'd rather not add another vendor.

**Where it falls short**

- These are loading features, not source connectors. Something else has to read your database or SaaS app and produce the files or rows.
- `COPY INTO` plus your own scripts means you write and run the scheduling, retries and schema handling yourself.
- [Snowflake's Snowpipe docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-load-snowpipe-intro) say it is very difficult for Snowflake to estimate Snowpipe latency. They also say that while Snowpipe generally loads older files first, there is no guarantee files load in the order they were staged.
- Snowpipe keeps each loaded file's path and name and will not load a file with the same name again, even if the file was changed afterwards ([Snowpipe docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-load-snowpipe-intro)).
- Snowflake recommends data files of roughly `100` to `250 MB` compressed, or larger. Snowpipe's cost includes an overhead for managing queued files that grows with the number of files waiting, so many small files raise the cost ([file preparation guide](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-load-considerations-prepare)).

#### 2. Snowflake Openflow: for teams who want Snowflake to run the connector

**At a glance**
- Billed by: vCPUs used by connector runtimes, per second with a 60-second minimum, for active runtimes only, per [Snowflake's Openflow cost page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/cost) (checked Oct 2026)
- Price: 0.0225 credits per vCPU-hour on BYOC (bring your own cloud), plus your cloud provider bill and Snowpipe charges for the data ([Openflow cost page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/cost)) (checked Oct 2026)
- Status: generally available. Snowflake's [Openflow overview](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/about) (checked Oct 2026) says Snowflake-run deployments are open to all accounts in AWS, Azure and Google Cloud Commercial regions, and BYOC deployments to AWS Commercial regions only. Its [connectors page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/connectors/about-openflow-connectors) still says AWS only

Openflow is "built on Apache NiFi", an open-source dataflow tool, and its connectors are curated NiFi flow definitions. [Snowflake's Openflow cost page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/cost) says database change data capture (CDC, reading a database's change log) connectors need a Snowflake warehouse. That covers both the first copy and the ongoing changes.

Connector families include MySQL, PostgreSQL, Oracle, SQL Server and MongoDB, plus SaaS apps and Kafka ([connectors page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/connectors/about-openflow-connectors)).

Snowflake's own [cost example](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/cost) adds up 84 vCPU-hours. That is 84 × 0.0225 = 1.89 credits, or about $3.78 at $2.00 a credit (checked Oct 2026). That is before the cloud bill for the servers.

**What's good**

- Two deployment types. In BYOC the data plane runs in your own cloud while Snowflake manages the service and control plane. The Snowflake deployment uses Snowpark Container Services ([Openflow overview](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/about)).
- The [connectors page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/connectors/about-openflow-connectors) lists SQL Server twice: a change tracking (CT) connector and a CDC connector that captures every row-level change. It also lists BigQuery with incremental change capture, Kafka and Kinesis Data Streams, and Salesforce Bulk API with incremental change detection.
- [Snowflake's cost page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/cost) says database CDC connectors need a warehouse for both the first snapshot and ongoing changes, and that you can schedule `MERGE` operations to manage that compute cost.
- The PostgreSQL connector can write Snowflake-managed Apache Iceberg tables as an opt-in format. Iceberg `v2` and `v3` are both supported, and storage can be Snowflake storage or an external volume in your own cloud storage ([PostgreSQL Iceberg page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/connectors/postgres/iceberg), a Preview feature).
- The MongoDB connector copies each selected collection in full once, then follows MongoDB change streams on a schedule ([MongoDB connector page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/connectors/mongodb/about)).

**Choose it if** you already pay Snowflake, run on AWS (or use a Snowflake-run deployment elsewhere), and like the idea of database CDC with one vendor and one contract.

**Where it falls short**

- The [connectors page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/connectors/about-openflow-connectors) says most listed connectors load data into Snowflake, so Openflow fits best when Snowflake is where your data ends up.
- The Oracle connector has its own licence in the [credit table](https://www.snowflake.com/legal-files/CreditConsumptionTable.pdf): $70 per licensed core per month on a 36-month commitment, plus support (checked Oct 2026).
- The [overview page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/about) says any new deployment is gen 2, and that gen 2 connector configuration remains in Public Preview.
- Openflow is not automatically available in trial accounts. The [overview page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/about) says to contact your Snowflake account team to enable it.
- The [PostgreSQL Iceberg page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/connectors/postgres/iceberg) carries a Preview Feature banner. Snowflake says not to change the `Table Storage Format` or the `Iceberg Version` after the connector starts.
- The [MongoDB connector page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-integration/openflow/connectors/mongodb/about) carries a Preview label. It does not support standalone MongoDB instances and relies on the `oplog`. It needs MongoDB `4.4` or later and supports username and password authentication only. Each document's whole payload lands in one `data` column next to its `id`.

### Managed ELT services you pay a vendor to run

#### 3. Fivetran: for vendor-run connectors

**At a glance**
- Billed by: monthly active rows (MAR), meaning rows that changed in a month, counted per connection
- Price: Free up to 500,000 MAR; $5 base charge for 1 to 1M MAR; Standard from $495 per million MAR, Enterprise $661.67, Business Critical $1,061.67 (per connection, list price, from its [Service Consumption Table](https://www.fivetran.com/legal/service-consumption-table)) (checked Oct 2026)
- Status: active; merged with dbt Labs on June 1, 2026

Fivetran counts rows that changed, and it counts them per connection. [Fivetran's pricing page](https://www.fivetran.com/pricing) lists Free, Standard, Enterprise and Business Critical plans (checked Oct 2026).

Its [Snowflake destination docs](https://fivetran.com/docs/destinations/snowflake) cover native tables, or Lakehouse tables (Beta), which are Iceberg tables that Fivetran manages in its own data lake service and Snowflake reads. The merger with dbt Labs is in [Fivetran's press release](https://www.fivetran.com/press/fivetran-dbt-labs-complete-merger-to-create-the-data-infrastructure-for-trusted-ai-agents).

**What's good**

- Its [sync overview](https://fivetran.com/docs/core-concepts/syncoverview) lists sync frequencies from 1 minute to 24 hours. The default is every 6 hours in fixed interval mode, and cron schedules are also offered.
- Its [database connectors docs](https://fivetran.com/docs/connectors/databases) say incremental changes come from the source's own change mechanism where one exists (MySQL and MariaDB binary log, PostgreSQL logical replication, MongoDB change streams, SQL Server CDC or change tracking), or from a Fivetran method such as Teleport Sync.
- The same [database connectors page](https://fivetran.com/docs/connectors/databases) says database connectors sync new and modified data every 15 minutes by default, update the destination in batches with a merge operation, and that most initial syncs finish within a day.
- [Fivetran's pricing page](https://www.fivetran.com/pricing) lists unlimited users, 700+ fully managed connectors and an Integration for dbt Core on Standard (checked Oct 2026).
- The same [pricing page](https://www.fivetran.com/pricing) lists 1-minute syncs, custom roles, SCIM and a Hybrid deployment option on Enterprise. Business Critical adds customer-managed keys, PCI DSS Level 1 certification and private networking options (checked Oct 2026).

**Choose it if** you want a vendor to own connector upkeep (Fivetran's pitch, not tested by us) for many SaaS apps and databases, and you'd rather pay a meter than hire an engineer.

**Where it falls short**

- The meter counts changed rows. Our read: a busy table nobody looks at can cost more than the summary table your dashboards actually use.
- Lakehouse tables are Beta, per [Fivetran's Snowflake destination docs](https://fivetran.com/docs/destinations/snowflake).
- The same [docs](https://fivetran.com/docs/destinations/snowflake) say the maximum length for `VARIANT` and `VARCHAR` columns is `128 MB`, and Fivetran truncates values that exceed it.
- The [pricing page](https://www.fivetran.com/pricing) lists 15-minute syncs for Standard. The [sync overview](https://fivetran.com/docs/core-concepts/syncoverview) says 1-minute syncs need an Enterprise or Business Critical plan and are not supported for any Lite connector (checked Oct 2026).
- The [pricing page](https://www.fivetran.com/pricing) FAQ counts Oracle and High-Volume Agent connectors as enterprise database connectors. The Enterprise plan lists that group, and Standard lists plain database connectors (checked Oct 2026).
- The [database connectors page](https://fivetran.com/docs/connectors/databases) says schema changes in the source are the most common trigger for a full table re-sync. It also says expired change logs require a full source re-sync, and that Teleport Sync may re-sync a whole table if you update large portions of it between syncs.

#### 4. Hevo: for small teams that want a free plan

**At a glance**
- Billed by: events, which count records inserted, updated or deleted at the source
- Price: Free is $0 for 1M events a month; Starter is $299 monthly or $265 billed yearly from 5M events, per [Hevo's pricing page](https://hevodata.com/pricing/) (checked Oct 2026)
- Status: active

[Hevo's pricing page](https://hevodata.com/pricing/) shows a free plan, a 14-day trial with no card, and Starter and Professional tiers (checked Oct 2026).

[Hevo's docs](https://docs.hevodata.com/destinations/) list Snowflake as a standard destination. A separate [Snowflake Iceberg destination](https://docs.hevodata.com/edge/destinations/snowflake-iceberg/) loads into Snowflake-managed Iceberg tables with files in your S3 bucket, and it's in Early Access.

The [same page](https://docs.hevodata.com/edge/destinations/snowflake-iceberg/) warns that Snowflake is deprecating password sign-in and enforcing multi-factor authentication, so Hevo recommends key pair authentication. Plan for it with any loader.

**What's good**

- [Hevo's log-based pipelines page](https://docs.hevodata.com/pipelines/working-with-pipelines/log-based-pipelines/) lists the change source for each database: BinLog for MySQL, logical replication for PostgreSQL, Change Streams or OpLog for MongoDB, Change Tracking for SQL Server and redo logs for Oracle.
- Per [Hevo's pricing page](https://hevodata.com/pricing/) (checked Oct 2026), Free covers limited connectors, up to 5 users and 1-hour scheduling. Starter, Professional and Business Critical list all available connectors.
- [Hevo's events usage docs](https://docs.hevodata.com/events-usage/understanding-events-usage-for-billing/) say that on plans from Release `1.48` onwards, going over the events quota starts a grace period to buy more events while pipelines keep running.
- Its [Snowflake Iceberg (Edge) docs](https://docs.hevodata.com/edge/destinations/snowflake-iceberg/) say the table data and metadata files sit in your own S3 bucket. They also say external engines such as Apache Spark and Trino can read the tables through the Horizon Iceberg REST Catalog API.

**Choose it if** you're a small team with common sources and want a free start.

**Where it falls short**

- [Hevo's events usage docs](https://docs.hevodata.com/events-usage/understanding-events-usage-for-billing/) say inserted and updated events at the source are billable, so a busy table costs more (a row changed five times is five events, our reading). They add that events deleted at the source are billable as updates.
- The [same docs](https://docs.hevodata.com/events-usage/understanding-events-usage-for-billing/) say Hevo charges the selected plan price whether or not you use the full capacity of the plan.
- The [log-based pipelines page](https://docs.hevodata.com/pipelines/working-with-pipelines/log-based-pipelines/) says streaming pipelines, available from Release `2.21`, cover those source types except SQL Server and DynamoDB, and that you must contact Hevo Sales to enable streaming.
- The [Iceberg docs](https://docs.hevodata.com/edge/destinations/snowflake-iceberg/) say the destination is currently available for Early Access and is enabled through a Hevo account executive or Support.
- The [Iceberg docs](https://docs.hevodata.com/edge/destinations/snowflake-iceberg/) list prerequisites of a Snowflake account on AWS, an S3 bucket with an IAM policy for Snowflake, and an external volume in Snowflake.

### Open source, source-available and streaming

#### 5. Airbyte: for a large connector catalog you can self-host

**At a glance**
- Billed by: credits on Cloud; nothing for Core, which you run yourself
- Price: Cloud Standard is $20 a month with 5 credits, then $5 per extra credit, per [Airbyte's Cloud release notes](https://docs.airbyte.com/release_notes/cloud) (the September 23, 2026 entry) (checked Oct 2026)
- Status: active

Airbyte Core is free to run yourself under the Elastic License 2.0 (per [Airbyte's licence FAQ](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/developer-guides/licenses/license-faq)). That licence does not allow offering the software to third parties as a hosted or managed service. Its [Snowflake destination](https://docs.airbyte.com/integrations/destinations/snowflake) loads through a Snowflake stage. [Airbyte's docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/integrations/destinations/snowflake) give the same advice as Fivetran's: a small, dedicated warehouse with a one-minute auto-suspend.

**What's good**

- [Airbyte's CDC docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/understanding-airbyte/cdc) list current support for Postgres, MySQL, Microsoft SQL Server, MongoDB, Oracle DB, SAP HANA and IBM Db2. The page does not say which plan or connector support level covers Oracle, SAP HANA and Db2.
- The [same docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/understanding-airbyte/cdc) say the first CDC sync takes a snapshot of the current data with a `SELECT`, and later syncs read the logs from the previously synced position.
- Cloud usage is metered in credits. Per [Airbyte's credits docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/cloud/managing-airbyte-cloud/manage-credits) (checked Oct 2026), databases and files use 4 credits per GB, and APIs and custom sources use 6 credits per million rows.
- The [credits docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/cloud/managing-airbyte-cloud/manage-credits) also say that if you run out of credits, Cloud keeps your syncs running and bills you in arrears. It does not stop in-progress syncs or pause scheduled ones. Trials last 14 days or 400 credits, whichever comes first (checked Oct 2026).
- Its [Snowflake destination docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/integrations/destinations/snowflake) say Airbyte loads through Snowflake's internal stage and supports Full Refresh Overwrite, Overwrite + Deduped and Append, plus Incremental Append and Append + Deduped.
- The [Cloud release notes](https://docs.airbyte.com/release_notes/cloud) list a Plus tier that starts at a 40-credit package, with a 15-minute sync frequency, two workspaces, single sign-on and premium support. They also say unused Cloud credits now roll over for 3 months (checked Oct 2026).

**Choose it if** you've got engineers and want a large connector catalog, with a cloud option later. Airbyte's [home page](https://airbyte.com/) says 700+ connectors and its [GitHub page](https://github.com/airbytehq/airbyte) says 600+; we did not count.

**Where it falls short**

- [Airbyte's CDC docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/understanding-airbyte/cdc) say CDC runs as scheduled syncs, not as a continuous stream.
- Self-hosting moves the bill to servers and staff, and [Airbyte's pricing page](https://airbyte.com/pricing) does not publish prices for Pro and Enterprise Flex (checked Oct 2026).
- The [CDC docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/understanding-airbyte/cdc) say CDC incremental sync works only for tables with primary keys for most sources. Changes made by `TRUNCATE` or `ALTER` do not appear in the logs, so they do not reach the destination, and a refresh is required after you add a new table to a CDC connection.
- [Airbyte's connector support levels page](https://docs.airbyte.com/integrations/connector-support-levels) says Marketplace connectors are maintained by community members, are not covered by Airbyte support SLAs, and may see breaking changes with no notice. Enterprise connectors are not available to Open Source or standard Cloud customers.
- [Airbyte's credits docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/cloud/managing-airbyte-cloud/manage-credits) say that for Full Refresh syncs of APIs and custom sources, every row synced is charged.
- The [Snowflake destination docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/integrations/destinations/snowflake) say Snowflake is enforcing strong authentication on a rolling per-account basis between August and October 2026. #### 10. dbt and dynamic tables: for SQL transforms after the load

**At a glance**
- Billed by: dbt charges developer seats and successful models built; dynamic tables use Snowflake credits
- Price: dbt Developer is free for 1 seat and `3,000` models a month; Starter is $100 per user per month, per [dbt's pricing page](https://www.getdbt.com/pricing) (checked Oct 2026)
- Status: active; dbt Labs merged with Fivetran on June 1, 2026

dbt turns SQL select statements into tested tables, and it needs a loader to run first. Snowflake's own answer is [dynamic tables](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/dynamic-tables-about), which keep the result of a query up to date with a minimum `target lag` of `60 seconds`. They cost warehouse compute, cloud services and storage, so the more often they refresh, the more you pay.

**What's good**

- [dbt's data tests docs](https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/build/data-tests) list four built-in generic tests: `unique`, `not_null`, `accepted_values` and `relationships`. Singular tests are plain SQL that returns the failing rows, and open-source packages such as `dbt-utils` and `dbt-expectations` add more generic tests.
- Per [dbt's pricing page](https://www.getdbt.com/pricing) (checked Oct 2026), Starter includes five developer seats, `15,000` successful models built a month and `5,000` queried metrics.
- [dbt's billing docs](https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/platform/billing/how-pricing-works) say only models that build successfully are counted. If a job of 100 models fails after 51 are built, 51 count.
- Snowflake runs managed dbt Core and dbt Fusion runtimes for dbt Projects on Snowflake. Executions use a virtual warehouse and standard compute costs, with no additional Snowflake licensing or per-user fees ([Snowflake's dbt Projects docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-engineering/dbt-projects-on-snowflake)).
- Snowflake's [dynamic tables docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/dynamic-tables-about) say they suit workloads that want results to stay up to date without custom orchestration code, and multi-step pipelines with joins, aggregations or window functions. It also says you can move from batch to near-real-time freshness by changing a single parameter, the `target lag`.

**Choose it if** your raw data is already in Snowflake and your team writes SQL. dbt suits tested, version-controlled projects, while dynamic tables suit teams that want no extra tool.

**Where it falls short**

- Neither one moves data into Snowflake. They sit idle until a loader has run.
- Both bring Snowflake compute cost that a loader-only price won't show you.
- Starter has 1 project, and the Enterprise and Enterprise+ plans are custom priced ([dbt pricing page](https://www.getdbt.com/pricing)) (checked Oct 2026).
- Snowflake's [dynamic tables page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/dynamic-tables-about) says they do not support workloads that need data fresher than `60 seconds` (the minimum `target lag`) or strictly guaranteed refresh timing.
- The [same page](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/dynamic-tables-about) says they do not support workloads that need stored procedures or external functions in the definition.

**What users say:** A PeerSpot reviewer on the [dbt reviews page](https://www.peerspot.com/products/dbt-reviews) calls version control "a huge bonus or a huge plus" (March 2026). Another says "Writing the scripts requires understanding Jinja technology" (April 2026). These are individual opinions; one person's view, with its date and link.

## Native Snowflake or a third-party tool?

Once your data is already in a file or a row, native loading adds no second vendor. We did not compare speed or total cost across tools. The split:

| Question | Native Snowflake can do it | You likely need another tool |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Load files already in cloud storage | Yes, `COPY INTO` or Snowpipe | No |
| Load rows from your own app or Kafka | Yes, Snowpipe Streaming or the Kafka connector | No |
| Read a SaaS app or database for you | Partly, through Openflow connectors | Yes, if the connector you need is not listed |
| Keep a SQL result fresh | Yes, dynamic tables | No, unless you want tested, versioned projects (dbt) |

Freshness, as the vendor pages put it:

- **Snowpipe Streaming** is described as "as low as 5 seconds" from ingest to queryable, with the caveat that results depend on workload ([Snowflake's Snowpipe Streaming overview docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-load-snowpipe-streaming-overview); we did not test it).
- **Snowpipe** makes data available within minutes after files reach a stage ([data loading overview](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/data-load-overview)).
- **Dynamic tables** have a minimum `target lag` of `60 seconds` ([Snowflake's dynamic tables docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/dynamic-tables-about)).
- **Hevo Free** schedules syncs every hour ([Hevo's pricing page](https://hevodata.com/pricing/)) (checked Oct 2026).

## How to choose: four questions

1. **Where does the data start?** Files or rows you already produce point to Snowpipe. SaaS apps point to a managed loader, and a live database points to change capture (see our [CDC tools comparison](/compare/best-change-data-capture-tools/)).
2. **How fresh must it be?** Pick the slowest schedule your readers will accept, because every faster sync adds warehouse time (see the worked example).
3. **Who runs it?** A vendor-run tool costs you a meter. Airbyte Core costs you servers and people.
4. **Do you need Iceberg?** Check the Iceberg column in the table first, and note which options are Beta or Early Access. OLake Go (entry 7) creates Iceberg tables in Snowflake, and [Where OLake Go fits](#where-olake-go-fits) covers Snowflake reading tables in your own storage.

## What to test before you sign

- **One busy table.** Load it for a week and count the rows, events or credits the vendor bills. Then compare that with the vendor's estimate.
- **Snowflake's side.** Read your own credit use for the load warehouse during the trial. The tool's invoice won't show it.
- **Deletes.** Check whether deleted rows arrive and whether they count as paid usage. Fivetran counts them as paid rows from January 2026, per its [pricing updates page](https://fivetran.com/docs/core-concepts/usage-based-pricing/pricing-updates/2026-pricing-updates) (checked Oct 2026).
- **Sign-in method.** Use key pair authentication from day one. [Hevo's](https://docs.hevodata.com/edge/destinations/snowflake-iceberg/) and [Airbyte's](https://docs.airbyte.com/integrations/destinations/snowflake) Snowflake docs both say Snowflake is moving away from password-only sign-in, so a password setup is a chore waiting to happen.

## Where OLake Go fits

OLake Go (entry 7) is our open-source ([Apache 2.0](https://github.com/datazip-inc/olake)) tool for copying databases into Apache Iceberg or Parquet on storage you control. From v0.11.0 it can also create Iceberg tables in Snowflake through Horizon.

Snowflake is also one of the query engines on [our compatibility page](/docs/understanding/compatibility-engines/). It reads OLake Go's Iceberg tables from the Glue, Lakekeeper, Unity, Polaris, S3 Tables, Nessie and BigLake catalogs and from generic REST catalogs, read-only for external catalogs.

See how to [load databases into Iceberg without Kafka](/compare/database-to-iceberg-without-kafka/).

## Conclusion

It depends on where your data starts and how fresh it has to be. Start with Snowflake's own loading if files or rows already exist, add a managed tool once you have many sources to connect, use OLake Go for open-source database replication into Iceberg, and pick a transform layer on its own.

Whatever you choose, price *both* bills. Then run a one-week test on your busiest table before you commit.
