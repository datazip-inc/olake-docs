## What is a data management platform?

Data management means looking after data from the day it's created until the day it's deleted.

The Data Management Body of Knowledge (DMBOK), a reference framework from a professional body for data managers, lists 11 areas. They include governance, architecture, security, integration, master data, metadata and data quality.

A data management platform is software that covers some of those areas in one product. A data management tool usually covers just one.

### The advertising meaning you may have landed on by mistake

Search this phrase and you'll get a pile of ad-tech results. In marketing, a DMP is a different product. [One IT software vendor](https://www.bmc.com/learn/data-management-platform.html) defines it as software that collects, organizes and deploys data from customers and audiences for use in online campaigns.

If that's what you're after, this guide won't help.

## How we picked

A tool had to be maintained, cover one of five jobs, and publish a product or pricing page we could open. We checked each vendor's own pages in October 2026 and link the page beside each price, plan or limit claim. You get a table first, then one block per tool in the same shape.

Vendor facts come from each vendor's own pages. We did not run the other tools. We quote no user reviews, and where a vendor shows no price we write "not published."

We left out advertising DMPs, dashboard tools such as Tableau, and tools whose pages we couldn't open. The order inside each group is not a ranking.

## Start from your problem

Most teams don't need all five jobs on day one. So ask which pain is real, then pick from that lane.

![Scattered data needs a pipeline and a store, while distrusted data needs a catalog, quality checks and master data](/img/blog/2026/10/best-data-management-platforms-start-from-problem.webp)

If people can't get to the data, you need a pipeline and a place to store it. If they can reach it but argue about what it means, you need a catalog, rules and a master record.

## Tools that move data

Moving data means copying it from apps and databases into that one place.

### 1. Fivetran: for teams that want a vendor to run the pipelines

**At a glance:** managed service, billed on monthly active rows (MAR), meaning rows that changed in a month.

Fivetran's [connector page](https://www.fivetran.com/connectors) says 750+ sources, while its [pricing page](https://www.fivetran.com/pricing) says 700+ fully managed connectors. The two pages disagree, so cite the page you use. Fivetran announced the completion of its merger with dbt Labs in a [press release](https://www.fivetran.com/press/fivetran-dbt-labs-complete-merger-to-create-the-data-infrastructure-for-trusted-ai-agents) on June 1, 2026 (checked Oct 2026).

**What's good**

- Its [pricing page](https://www.fivetran.com/pricing) lists Free, Standard, Enterprise and Business Critical plans, with a free plan of up to 500,000 MAR a month (checked Oct 2026).
- That page says Standard includes 15-minute syncs, unlimited users, REST API access, SSH tunnels and an integration for dbt Core ([pricing page](https://www.fivetran.com/pricing)) (checked Oct 2026).
- It says Enterprise adds 1-minute syncs, custom roles, SCIM user provisioning and a Hybrid deployment option ([pricing page](https://www.fivetran.com/pricing)) (checked Oct 2026). We did not test the sync times, so ask which plan your connector gets.
- Its [Managed Data Lake Service docs](https://fivetran.com/docs/destinations/managed-data-lake-service) say Fivetran writes Parquet files to your own cloud storage on Amazon S3, ADLS or GCS and always creates Iceberg metadata for the tables. The limits are below (checked Oct 2026).

**Where it falls short**

- Paid per-row rates sit in a separate [Service Consumption Table](https://www.fivetran.com/legal/service-consumption-table), so your bill depends on your row counts and connections (checked Oct 2026).
- Its [destinations docs](https://fivetran.com/docs/destinations) say your own warehouse provider charges separately for the compute Fivetran uses to load data (checked Oct 2026).
- The [pricing page](https://www.fivetran.com/pricing) applies a $5 base charge to standard connections with monthly usage between 1 MAR and 1 million MAR (checked Oct 2026).
- That page says usage counts updates, which it defines as changes to existing rows in your destination, including deletes ([pricing page](https://www.fivetran.com/pricing)) (checked Oct 2026).
- The [Managed Data Lake Service docs](https://fivetran.com/docs/destinations/managed-data-lake-service) say Fivetran does not support position deletes for Iceberg tables. They also say it does not support the S3 Glacier Flexible Retrieval and S3 Glacier Deep Archive storage tiers, because retrieval takes too long (checked Oct 2026).

### 2. Airbyte: for teams that want open-source connectors

**At a glance:** open source with a self-run Core edition, plus a paid Cloud service billed in credits.

Airbyte's [platform docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/) say it has over 600 pre-built connectors, while its [why-open-source page](https://airbyte.com/why-open-source) says 700+ (another small mismatch). Its [pricing page](https://airbyte.com/pricing) shows Cloud Standard at $20 a month with 5 credits, then $5 per extra credit (checked Oct 2026).

**What's good**

- Its [platform docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/) say it is available as a self-managed, hybrid or fully managed cloud solution.
- They say you can deploy Core locally or in your own infrastructure, and the [why-open-source page](https://airbyte.com/why-open-source) says to self-host it with `abctl` or Kubernetes (checked Oct 2026).
- The [pricing page](https://airbyte.com/pricing) shows Plus starting at $189 a month for a 40-credit package, plus a 14-day free trial with 400 credits (checked Oct 2026).
- The Plus card on the [pricing page](https://airbyte.com/pricing) lists enterprise support with a 24-hour response SLA (checked Oct 2026).

**Where it falls short**

- Change data capture (CDC) means copying only the rows that changed. Airbyte's [CDC docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/understanding-airbyte/cdc) say syncs are scheduled, and "We do not treat CDC sources as infinite streaming sources." (checked Oct 2026)
- The same [CDC docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/understanding-airbyte/cdc) say CDC incremental is supported only for tables with primary keys for most sources (checked Oct 2026).
- They also say changes made by `TRUNCATE` or `ALTER` do not appear in the logs, so they never reach the destination ([CDC docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/understanding-airbyte/cdc)) (checked Oct 2026).
- A refresh is required after you add a new table or collection to a CDC connection, per the [same page](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/understanding-airbyte/cdc) (checked Oct 2026).
- The [pricing page](https://airbyte.com/pricing)'s comparison table lists the maximum sync frequency as 1 hour on Standard, 15 minutes on Plus and 5 minutes on Pro and Enterprise Flex. Pro and Enterprise Flex use capacity-based pricing, and the page shows no price for them (checked Oct 2026).
- The [why-open-source page](https://airbyte.com/why-open-source) lists the connectors and core platform under the Elastic License 2.0 (ELv2), which it describes as restricted only for resale as a hosted service (checked Oct 2026).

### 3. OLake Go: for teams that want to copy databases into Iceberg tables they own

<img src="/img/logo/olake-blue-with-text.svg" alt="OLake" height="32" style={{display: 'block', margin: '2px 0 14px'}} />

**At a glance:** open-source database replication under [Apache 2.0](https://github.com/datazip-inc/olake). It copies Postgres, MySQL/MariaDB, MongoDB and SQL Server with a full load and then change capture ([Postgres](/docs/connectors/postgres/), [MySQL](/docs/connectors/mysql/), [MongoDB](/docs/connectors/mongodb/), [SQL Server](/docs/connectors/mssql/)), and writes Apache Iceberg or Parquet.

OLake Go handles the move step, so pair it with the tools in the later groups for catalog, quality and master data. For support, [contact the OLake team](/contact/).

**What's good**

- The [docs overview](/docs/) lists, per source, full refresh, full refresh plus CDC, CDC only and full refresh plus incremental sync modes, so one tool does the first load and then the change capture.
- [Oracle](/docs/connectors/oracle/), [Db2](/docs/connectors/db2/), [S3](/docs/connectors/s3/) and [Kafka](/docs/connectors/kafka/) also work as sources, without database change capture.
- It writes Iceberg tables to catalogs such as [AWS Glue](/docs/writers/iceberg/catalog/glue/), with eleven catalog types on the [catalog compatibility page](/docs/understanding/compatibility-catalogs/), or Parquet files on object storage.
- Those catalogs include [Snowflake Horizon](/docs/writers/iceberg/catalog/rest/?rest-catalog=horizon) (OLake Go v0.11.0 and later), [BigLake](/docs/writers/iceberg/catalog/rest/?rest-catalog=biglake) for Google Cloud and [Unity Catalog](/docs/writers/iceberg/catalog/rest/?rest-catalog=unity) for Databricks.
- Schema discovery runs at the start of every sync, and a new source column is added to the Iceberg schema automatically when **Sync new columns automatically** is on ([schema evolution docs](/docs/features/schema/)).
- You run it on your own infrastructure with [Docker](/docs/install/docker-cli/), [Helm on Kubernetes](/docs/install/kubernetes/) or the [OLake UI](/docs/install/olake-ui/).
- OLake Fusion, a companion product, compacts the Iceberg tables OLake Go writes and shows a health score per table ([Fusion docs](/docs/fusion/getting-started/configure-first-compaction/)).

## Tools that store and query data

A data warehouse is a database built for reports. Lakehouses keep data in cheap file storage but let you query it like a warehouse. All three tools below say they work with the open Apache Iceberg table format: see the Iceberg docs of [Snowflake](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/tables-iceberg), [BigQuery](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/iceberg-tables) and [Databricks](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/iceberg/).

### 4. Snowflake: for teams that want a vendor-managed warehouse

**At a glance:** a managed cloud data platform with a consumption-based price.

Snowflake's [pricing page](https://www.snowflake.com/en/pricing-options/) calls its model consumption-based (its own wording: it says it keeps pricing simple), and describes Standard as an entry-level, introductory offering. Its [Iceberg docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/tables-iceberg) say Iceberg tables combine Snowflake's query features with external cloud storage that you manage.

**What's good**

- Snowflake sells four editions: Standard, Enterprise, Business Critical and Virtual Private Snowflake (VPS), which the [pricing page](https://www.snowflake.com/en/pricing-options/) says includes all Business Critical features in a completely separate environment (checked Oct 2026).
- The [pricing page](https://www.snowflake.com/en/pricing-options/) says the monthly fee for stored data uses the average amount stored per month, after compression (checked Oct 2026).
- The [Iceberg docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/tables-iceberg) say Snowflake supports Iceberg tables that use Parquet files and versions `1`, `2` and `3` of the Iceberg specification, excluding row-level equality deletes (checked Oct 2026).
- They list two catalog options ([Iceberg docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/tables-iceberg)): Snowflake as the Iceberg catalog, which gives full platform support with read and write access, or an external catalog such as a remote Iceberg REST catalog, including AWS Glue and Snowflake Open Catalog.
- They also say Snowflake supports [externally managed writes](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/tables-iceberg-externally-managed-writes) for Iceberg tables that use specification version `2` or `3` (checked Oct 2026).

**Where it falls short**

- The [credit consumption table](https://www.snowflake.com/legal-files/CreditConsumptionTable.pdf) (Table 2(a), effective October 15, 2026) lists $2.00 (Standard), $3.00 (Enterprise) and $4.00 (Business Critical) per credit for AWS, US East (Northern Virginia) (checked Oct 2026). The [pricing page](https://www.snowflake.com/en/pricing-options/) has a platform and region picker, so your rate may differ, and a credit rate is not a monthly bill.
- Rates move with region: Table 2(a) of the [credit consumption table](https://www.snowflake.com/legal-files/CreditConsumptionTable.pdf) lists AWS EU Frankfurt at $2.60, $3.90 and $5.20 for the same three editions, and Table 3(a) lists AWS US East storage at $23.00 per terabyte per month on demand (checked Oct 2026).
- Pre-paid capacity is priced as the on-demand rate with a discount set in your order form, and the [table](https://www.snowflake.com/legal-files/CreditConsumptionTable.pdf) does not publish the discount percentages (checked Oct 2026).
- Per the [Iceberg docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/tables-iceberg), an Iceberg table that uses an external catalog gets limited Snowflake platform support. Row-level deletes work only as position deletes on version `2` tables and as deletion vectors on version `3` tables (checked Oct 2026).
- If your Snowflake account and external volume sit in different regions, your external cloud storage account incurs egress costs when you query the table ([Iceberg docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/tables-iceberg)) (checked Oct 2026).

### 5. Google BigQuery: for teams already on Google Cloud

**At a glance:** a serverless warehouse. A TiB, the unit it bills queries in by default, is about a trillion bytes.

**What's good**

- The [pricing page](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/pricing) says queries are billed per TiB scanned by default and "The first 1 TiB of query data processed per month is free" (checked Oct 2026).
- The first `10 GiB` of storage is also free each month ([pricing page](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/pricing)) (checked Oct 2026).
- Long-term storage covers any table or partition not modified for 90 consecutive days, and its storage price drops automatically by about 50% ([pricing page](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/pricing)) (checked Oct 2026).
- Capacity pricing is available in 3 editions, Standard, Enterprise and Enterprise Plus, and the Standard edition lists slots at $0.04 per hour with no commitment (default view, and the price varies by region and edition) ([pricing page](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/pricing)) (checked Oct 2026).
- Its [Iceberg docs](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/iceberg-tables) say Iceberg managed tables keep data in customer-owned storage buckets and support DML, schema evolution (add, drop and rename columns) and automatic storage optimization.
- They also list an export of an Iceberg `V2` snapshot, refreshed on each table mutation, for engines such as Spark.

**Where it falls short**

- With on-demand pricing, "you will generally have access to up to 2,000 concurrent slots, shared among all queries in a single project," so busy projects share capacity ([pricing page](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/pricing)) (checked Oct 2026).
- On-demand charges round up to the nearest MB, with a minimum of `10 MB` per table referenced and per query, and you pay for the data in the columns you select even when you set a `LIMIT` ([pricing page](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/pricing)) (checked Oct 2026).
- The rendered on-demand rate after the free TiB is $6.25 per TiB, but the table text does not name its region, so check yours ([pricing page](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/pricing)) (checked Oct 2026).
- Per the [Iceberg docs](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/iceberg-tables), only one mutating DML statement (`UPDATE`, `DELETE` or `MERGE`) runs at a time for each Iceberg managed table, and others queue (checked Oct 2026).
- Iceberg managed tables do not support renaming operations, table copies or table clones ([Iceberg docs](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/iceberg-tables)) (checked Oct 2026).

### 6. Databricks: for teams that mix analytics with machine learning

**At a glance:** a data and AI platform on AWS, Azure and Google Cloud, billed pay as you go.

**What's good**

- The [pricing page](https://www.databricks.com/product/pricing) says "Only pay for the products you use at per second granularity," with no up-front costs (checked Oct 2026).
- It offers committed-use contracts, where larger commitments earn greater discounts, and lists a Free Edition ([pricing page](https://www.databricks.com/product/pricing)) (checked Oct 2026).
- Its [Iceberg docs](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/iceberg/) say it supports Iceberg tables that use Parquet files and versions `1`, `2` and `3` of the Iceberg specification (the format's own version numbers) (checked Oct 2026).
- They say it supports Iceberg tables managed by Unity Catalog and by foreign catalogs, such as AWS Glue, Hive metastore or Snowflake Horizon Catalog ([Iceberg docs](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/iceberg/)).
- Iceberg tables you create in Unity Catalog are managed Iceberg tables ([Iceberg docs](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/iceberg/)), and you can create them with Databricks Runtime, Databricks SQL or external engines that support the Iceberg REST Catalog API, such as Apache Spark, Flink, Trino or Kafka.

**Where it falls short**

- Iceberg tables need Unity Catalog and Databricks Runtime `16.4` LTS (long-term support) or above, and managed tables also need serverless compute ([Iceberg docs](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/iceberg/)) (checked Oct 2026).
- Foreign Iceberg tables are read-only in Databricks and have limited platform support ([Iceberg docs](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/iceberg/)) (checked Oct 2026).
- Credential vending is not supported on workspaces that use default storage ([Iceberg docs](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/iceberg/)) (checked Oct 2026).
- Partition evolution on managed Iceberg tables works through external Iceberg engines using the Iceberg REST Catalog, but not through Databricks SQL ([Iceberg docs](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/iceberg/)) (checked Oct 2026).
- Serverless compute must have network connectivity to the cloud storage account that backs a managed Iceberg table ([Iceberg docs](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/iceberg/)) (checked Oct 2026).
- The [pricing page](https://www.databricks.com/product/pricing) text we read lists no editions beyond Free Edition and pay as you go (checked Oct 2026).

## Tools that catalog and govern data

A data catalog is a searchable inventory of your data, with owners and descriptions. Data governance is the set of rules about who may use data and how. Lineage is a record of where a piece of data came from.

### 7. Microsoft Purview: for teams that already run on Microsoft

**At a glance:** Microsoft's [Learn page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/purview) says Purview helps organizations govern, protect and manage data. It groups security, governance and compliance products, and its governance pieces are Data Map and Unified Catalog.

**What's good**

- The [Learn page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/purview) says the Purview portal provides access to data governance, data security and data compliance solutions in one place.
- It lists the data security solutions ([Learn page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/purview)) as Data Loss Prevention, Data Security Investigations, Information Barriers, Information Protection and Insider Risk Management.
- The [Azure pricing page](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/purview/) bills Unified Catalog per governed asset, and the monthly charge for a managed asset is prorated by the days it is governed in the billing cycle (checked Oct 2026).
- Governed assets can be tables, views, AI models and semantic models that are linked to governance concepts ([pricing page](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/purview/)).
- A Data Map starts at 1 Capacity Unit, which supports up to `25` operations per second and up to `10 GB` of metadata ([pricing page](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/purview/)) (checked Oct 2026).

**Where it falls short**

- Per-unit rates didn't show in the text of the [pricing page](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/purview/) we fetched. Its own example says 200 governed assets cost $100 for 30 days without discounts (checked Oct 2026).
- Only assets linked to governance concepts count as governed assets, and the Data Map needs an additional Capacity Unit for every `10 GB` of metadata storage ([pricing page](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/purview/)) (checked Oct 2026).
- Governing data in other clouds such as AWS or GCP may incur data transfer and API call charges ([pricing page](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/purview/)) (checked Oct 2026).
- Purview provisions a storage account and an Azure Event Hubs account as managed resources in your subscription, which may carry separate charges that in most cases will not exceed 2% of scanning charges ([pricing page](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/purview/)) (checked Oct 2026).
- Microsoft's [Learn page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/purview) lists Data Security Posture Management as in preview (checked Oct 2026).

### 8. Collibra: for large teams with formal governance programs

## Where all-in-one suites fit

Fivetran's [guide on its site](https://www.fivetran.com/learn/data-management-tools) argues that unified tools reduce the work of running many point tools. That is a vendor view. The same page calls its own list "specialized data management tools you can assemble alongside others."

Choose a suite if you already have one vendor, a budget for a contract and few engineers to wire tools together. Choose separate tools if you want open source, a lower starting cost or the freedom to swap one part later.

## A zero-licence starting stack (and what it still costs)

Here's one way to start with no licence fees, using only prices shown above. Run Airbyte Core yourself, query in BigQuery under its free 1 TiB of queries a month ([BigQuery pricing](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/pricing)) (checked Oct 2026), catalog with OpenMetadata, and check rules with Great Expectations. The software price is $0 for each of those pieces on the free terms listed above.

"No licence" does not mean "free." You still pay for servers, for BigQuery storage and any queries past the free tier, and for the engineers who keep four tools working. A paid starting point is Airbyte Cloud Standard at [$20 a month with 5 credits](https://airbyte.com/pricing) (checked Oct 2026), which swaps the server work for a bill.

## How to choose a data management platform

1. **Name the pain.** Data people can't reach needs a pipeline and a store. Data they don't trust needs a catalog, quality checks or master data.
2. **Check the meter.** Rows, credits, scanned bytes, governed assets and processing units all grow differently, and that's where bills surprise people.
3. **Count who will run it.** Open-source tools are free to license, but they still need servers and people.
4. **Ask for the missing price in writing.** Collibra, Semarchy and Informatica each want you to ask for a quote.
5. **Test one tool before adding a second.** Add jobs once the first one works.

Snowflake, BigQuery and Databricks all document support for Apache Iceberg, an open table format for data kept in your own storage (see the Iceberg docs of [Snowflake](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/tables-iceberg), [BigQuery](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/docs/iceberg-tables) and [Databricks](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/iceberg/)). If you keep data there, the copy step matters a lot. OLake Go, covered in the first group above, writes Iceberg tables through catalogs those platforms use: [Snowflake Horizon](/docs/writers/iceberg/catalog/rest/?rest-catalog=horizon), [BigLake](/docs/writers/iceberg/catalog/rest/?rest-catalog=biglake) and [Unity Catalog](/docs/writers/iceberg/catalog/rest/?rest-catalog=unity).

## The short version, again

A data management platform is a set of jobs: move the data, store it, find it, check it and agree on one master record. Match each job to a tool, and add them in the order your problems show up.

The cheapest way to start is usually a free tier or an open-source tool for the first job. If a vendor doesn't publish a price, ask for a written quote before you commit.
