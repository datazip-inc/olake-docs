## What is a data management platform?

Data management means looking after data from the day it's created until the day it's deleted.

The Data Management Body of Knowledge (DMBOK), a reference framework from a professional body for data managers, lists 11 areas. They include governance, architecture, security, integration, master data, metadata and data quality.

A data management platform is software that covers some of those areas in one product. A data management tool? That usually covers just one.

### The advertising meaning you may have landed on by mistake

Search this phrase and you'll get a pile of ad-tech results. In marketing, a DMP is a different product. One IT software vendor defines it as software that collects, organizes and deploys data from customers and audiences for use in online campaigns.

If that's what you're after, this guide won't help.

Most teams don't need all five jobs on day one. So ask which pain is real, then pick from that lane.

## Tools that move data

Moving data means copying it from apps and databases into that one place.

### 1. Fivetran: for teams that want a vendor to run the pipelines

![Fivetran home page showing its headline and main navigation](/img/blog/2026/10/landing-fivetran.webp)

*Fivetran home page, October 2026.*

**At a glance:** managed service, billed on monthly active rows (MAR), meaning rows that changed in a month.

Fivetran's connector page says 750+ sources, while its [pricing page](https://www.fivetran.com/pricing) says 700+ fully managed connectors. Yes, they disagree, so cite the page you use. Fivetran announced the completion of its merger with dbt Labs in a press release on June 1, 2026.

**What's good**

- Its pricing page lists a free plan of up to 500,000 MAR a month.
- That page says Standard includes 15-minute syncs, unlimited users, REST API access, SSH tunnels and an integration for dbt Core.
- It says Enterprise adds 1-minute syncs, custom roles, SCIM user provisioning and a Hybrid deployment option. We did not test the sync times, so ask which plan your connector gets.
- Its [Managed Data Lake Service docs](https://fivetran.com/docs/destinations/managed-data-lake-service) say Fivetran writes Parquet files to your own cloud storage on Amazon S3, ADLS or GCS and always creates Iceberg metadata for the tables. The limits are below.

**Where it falls short**

- Paid per-row rates sit in a separate [Service Consumption Table](https://www.fivetran.com/legal/service-consumption-table), not on the pricing page, so your bill depends on your row counts and connections.
- Its destinations docs say your own warehouse provider charges separately for the compute Fivetran uses to load data.
- The pricing page applies a $5 base charge to standard connections with monthly usage between 1 MAR and 1 million MAR.
- That page says usage counts updates, which it defines as changes to existing rows in your destination, including deletes.
- The Managed Data Lake Service docs say Fivetran does not support position deletes for Iceberg tables. They also say it does not support the S3 Glacier Flexible Retrieval and S3 Glacier Deep Archive storage tiers, because retrieval takes too long.

![Fivetran pricing page showing Free, Standard, Enterprise and Business Critical plan cards, with free up to 500,000 monthly active rows](/img/blog/2026/10/fivetran-pricing-plans.webp)

*Fivetran pricing page, October 2026.*

### 2. Airbyte: for teams that want open-source connectors

![Airbyte home page showing its headline and main navigation](/img/blog/2026/10/landing-airbyte.webp)

*Airbyte home page, October 2026.*

**At a glance:** open source with a self-run Core edition, plus a paid Cloud service billed in credits.

Airbyte's platform docs say it has over 600 pre-built connectors, while its why-open-source page says 700+ (another small mismatch). Its [pricing page](https://airbyte.com/pricing) shows Cloud Standard at $20 a month with 5 credits, then $5 per extra credit.

**What's good**

- Its platform docs say it is available as a self-managed, hybrid or fully managed cloud solution.
- They describe Core as the free and open-source version, which you can deploy locally or in your own infrastructure, and the why-open-source page says to self-host it with abctl or Kubernetes.
- The pricing page shows Plus starting at $189 a month for a 40-credit package, plus a 14-day free trial with 400 credits.
- The Plus card lists enterprise support with a 24-hour response SLA.

**Where it falls short**

- Change data capture (CDC) means copying only the rows that changed. Airbyte's [CDC docs](https://docs.airbyte.com/platform/understanding-airbyte/cdc) say syncs are scheduled, and "We do not treat CDC sources as infinite streaming sources."
- The same docs say CDC incremental is supported only for tables with primary keys for most sources.
- They also say changes made by TRUNCATE or ALTER do not appear in the logs, so they never reach the destination.
- A refresh is required after you add a new table or collection to a CDC connection, per the same page.
- The pricing page's comparison table lists the maximum sync frequency as 1 hour on Standard, 15 minutes on Plus and 5 minutes on Pro and Enterprise Flex. Pro and Enterprise Flex use capacity-based pricing, and the page shows no price for them.
- The why-open-source page lists the connectors and core platform under the Elastic License 2.0 (ELv2), which it describes as restricted only for resale as a hosted service.

![Airbyte pricing page showing Standard at $20 a month with 5 credits and extra credits at $5 each, plus Plus, Pro and Enterprise Flex](/img/blog/2026/10/best-data-management-platforms--airbyte-pricing-plans.webp)

*Airbyte pricing page, October 2026.*

### 3. OLake Go: for teams that want to copy databases into Iceberg tables they own

<img src="/img/logo/olake-blue-with-text.svg" alt="OLake" height="32" style={{display: 'block', margin: '2px 0 14px'}} />

![OLake home page showing its headline and main navigation](/img/blog/2026/10/landing-olake.webp)

*OLake home page, October 2026.*

**At a glance:** open-source database replication under [Apache 2.0](https://github.com/datazip-inc/olake). It copies Postgres, MySQL/MariaDB, MongoDB and SQL Server with a full load and then change capture ([Postgres](/docs/connectors/postgres/), [MySQL](/docs/connectors/mysql/), [MongoDB](/docs/connectors/mongodb/), [SQL Server](/docs/connectors/mssql/)), and writes Apache Iceberg or Parquet.

OLake Go moves data. It is not a catalog, quality or master data tool, so pair it with the tools in the later groups. For support, [contact the OLake team](/contact/).

**What's good**

- The [docs overview](/docs/) lists, per source, full refresh, full refresh plus CDC, CDC only and full refresh plus incremental sync modes, so one tool does the first load and then the change capture.
- [Oracle](/docs/connectors/oracle/), [Db2](/docs/connectors/db2/), [S3](/docs/connectors/s3/) and [Kafka](/docs/connectors/kafka/) also work as sources, without database change capture.
- It writes Iceberg tables to catalogs such as [AWS Glue](/docs/writers/iceberg/catalog/glue/), with eleven catalog types on the [catalog compatibility page](/docs/understanding/compatibility-catalogs/), or Parquet files on object storage.
- Schema discovery runs at the start of every sync, and a new source column is added to the Iceberg schema automatically ([schema evolution docs](/docs/features/schema/)).
- You run it on your own infrastructure with [Docker](/docs/install/docker-cli/), [Helm on Kubernetes](/docs/install/kubernetes/) or the [OLake UI](/docs/install/olake-ui/).

## Tools that store and query data

A data warehouse is a database built for reports. Lakehouses keep data in cheap file storage but let you query it like a warehouse. All three tools below say they work with the open Apache Iceberg table format: see the Iceberg docs of Snowflake, BigQuery and Databricks.

### 4. Snowflake: for teams that want a vendor-managed warehouse

![Snowflake home page showing its headline and main navigation](/img/blog/2026/10/landing-snowflake.webp)

*Snowflake home page, October 2026.*

**At a glance:** a managed cloud data platform with a consumption-based price.

Snowflake's pricing page calls its model consumption-based (its own wording: it says it keeps pricing simple), and describes Standard as an entry-level, introductory offering. Its Iceberg docs say Iceberg tables combine Snowflake's query features with external cloud storage that you manage.

**What's good**

- Snowflake sells four editions: Standard, Enterprise, Business Critical and Virtual Private Snowflake (VPS), which the pricing page says includes all Business Critical features in a completely separate environment.
- The pricing page says the monthly fee for stored data uses the average amount stored per month, after compression.
- The Iceberg docs say Snowflake supports Iceberg tables that use Parquet files and versions 1, 2 and 3 of the Iceberg specification, excluding row-level equality deletes.
- They list two catalog options: Snowflake as the Iceberg catalog, which gives full platform support with read and write access, or an external catalog such as a remote Iceberg REST catalog, including AWS Glue and Snowflake Open Catalog.
- They also say Snowflake supports externally managed writes for Iceberg tables that use specification version 2 or 3.

**Where it falls short**

- The pricing page showed $2.00 (Standard), $3.00 (Enterprise) and $4.00 (Business Critical) per credit for AWS, US East (Northern Virginia) when we read it in a browser in October 2026, and the [credit consumption table](https://www.snowflake.com/legal-files/CreditConsumptionTable.pdf) (Table 2(a), effective October 2, 2026) lists the same rates. There is a platform and region picker, so your rate may differ, and a credit rate is not a monthly bill.
- Rates move with region: Table 2(a) lists AWS EU Frankfurt at $2.60, $3.90 and $5.20 for the same three editions, and Table 3(a) lists AWS US East storage at $23.00 per terabyte per month on demand.
- Pre-paid capacity is priced as the on-demand rate with a discount set in your order form, and the table does not publish the discount percentages.
- Per the Iceberg docs, an Iceberg table that uses an external catalog gets limited Snowflake platform support. Row-level deletes work only as position deletes on version 2 tables and as deletion vectors on version 3 tables.
- If your Snowflake account and external volume sit in different regions, your external cloud storage account incurs egress costs when you query the table.
- No self-hosted option is stated.

![Snowflake pricing options page showing the Standard, Enterprise and Business Critical editions at 2, 3 and 4 dollars per credit](/img/blog/2026/10/best-data-management-platforms--snowflake-pricing-options.webp)

*Snowflake pricing page, October 2026.*

### 5. Google BigQuery: for teams already on Google Cloud

![Google BigQuery home page showing its headline and main navigation](/img/blog/2026/10/landing-bigquery.webp)

*Google BigQuery home page, October 2026.*

**At a glance:** a serverless warehouse. A TiB, the unit it bills queries in by default, is about a trillion bytes.

**What's good**

- The [pricing page](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/pricing) says queries are billed per TiB scanned by default and "The first 1 TiB of query data processed per month is free."
- The first 10 GiB of storage is also free each month.
- Long-term storage covers any table or partition not modified for 90 consecutive days, and its storage price drops automatically by about 50%.
- Capacity pricing is available in 3 editions, Standard, Enterprise and Enterprise Plus, and the Standard edition lists slots at $0.04 per hour with no commitment (default view, and the price varies by region and edition).
- Its Iceberg docs say Iceberg managed tables keep data in customer-owned storage buckets and support DML, schema evolution (add, drop and rename columns) and automatic storage optimization.
- They also list an export of an Iceberg V2 snapshot, refreshed on each table mutation, for engines such as Spark.

**Where it falls short**

- On-demand queries get "generally ... access to up to 2,000 concurrent slots, shared among all queries in a single project," so busy projects share capacity.
- On-demand charges round up to the nearest MB, with a minimum of 10 MB per table referenced and per query, and you pay for the data in the columns you select even when you set a LIMIT.
- The rendered on-demand rate after the free TiB is $6.25 per TiB, but the table text does not name its region, so check yours.
- Per the Iceberg docs, only one mutating DML statement (UPDATE, DELETE or MERGE) runs at a time for each Iceberg managed table, and others queue.
- Iceberg managed tables do not support renaming operations, table copies or table clones.
- It's a proprietary Google Cloud service, with no self-hosted option stated.

![BigQuery pricing page table of monthly free usage limits showing the first 1 TiB of query data each month is free](/img/blog/2026/10/best-data-management-platforms--bigquery-on-demand-free-tib.webp)

*Google BigQuery pricing page, October 2026.*

### 6. Databricks: for teams that mix analytics with machine learning

![Databricks home page showing its headline and main navigation](/img/blog/2026/10/landing-databricks.webp)

*Databricks home page, October 2026.*

**At a glance:** a data and AI platform on AWS, Azure and Google Cloud, billed pay as you go.

**What's good**

- The [pricing page](https://www.databricks.com/product/pricing) says "Only pay for the products you use at per second granularity," with no up-front costs.
- It offers committed-use contracts, where larger commitments earn greater discounts, and lists a Free Edition.
- Its Iceberg docs say it supports Iceberg tables that use Parquet files and versions 1, 2 and 3 of the Iceberg specification (the format's own version numbers).
- They say it supports Iceberg tables managed by Unity Catalog and by foreign catalogs, such as AWS Glue, Hive metastore or Snowflake Horizon Catalog.
- Iceberg tables you create in Unity Catalog are managed Iceberg tables, and you can create them with Databricks Runtime, Databricks SQL or external engines that support the Iceberg REST Catalog API, such as Apache Spark, Flink, Trino or Kafka.

**Where it falls short**

- Iceberg tables need Unity Catalog and Databricks Runtime 16.4 LTS (long-term support) or above, and managed tables also need serverless compute.
- Foreign Iceberg tables are read-only in Databricks and have limited platform support.
- Credential vending is not supported on workspaces that use default storage.
- Partition evolution on managed Iceberg tables works through external Iceberg engines using the Iceberg REST Catalog, but not through Databricks SQL.
- Serverless compute must have network connectivity to the cloud storage account that backs a managed Iceberg table.
- The pricing page text we read does not list editions beyond Free Edition and pay as you go.

## Tools that catalog and govern data

A data catalog is a searchable inventory of your data, with owners and descriptions. Data governance is the set of rules about who may use data and how. Lineage is a record of where a piece of data came from.

### 7. Microsoft Purview: for teams that already run on Microsoft

![Microsoft Purview product page showing its headline and main navigation](/img/blog/2026/10/landing-microsoft-purview.webp)

*Microsoft Purview product page, October 2026.*

**At a glance:** Microsoft's Learn page says Purview helps organizations govern, protect and manage data. It groups security, governance and compliance products, and its governance pieces are Data Map and Unified Catalog.

**What's good**

- The Learn page says the Purview portal provides access to data governance, data security and data compliance solutions in one place.
- It lists the data security solutions as Data Loss Prevention, Data Security Investigations, Information Barriers, Information Protection and Insider Risk Management.
- The [Azure pricing page](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/purview/) bills Unified Catalog per governed asset, and the monthly charge for a managed asset is prorated by the days it is governed in the billing cycle.
- Governed assets can be tables, views, AI models and semantic models that are linked to governance concepts.
- A Data Map starts at 1 Capacity Unit, which supports up to 25 operations per second and up to 10 GB of metadata.

**Where it falls short**

- Per-unit rates didn't show in the pricing page text we fetched. Its own example says 200 governed assets cost $100 for 30 days without discounts.
- Only assets linked to governance concepts count as governed assets, and the Data Map needs an additional Capacity Unit for every 10 GB of metadata storage.
- Governing data in other clouds such as AWS or GCP may incur data transfer and API call charges.
- Purview provisions a storage account and an Azure Event Hubs account as managed resources in your subscription, which may carry separate charges that in most cases will not exceed 2% of scanning charges.
- Microsoft's Learn page lists Data Security Posture Management as in preview.

### 11. Great Expectations: for teams that write Python

![Great Expectations home page showing its headline and main navigation](/img/blog/2026/10/landing-great-expectations.webp)

**At a glance:** the Great Expectations docs say its Core library is a Python library for building and running data validation workflows, built around rules called expectations. The repository licence is Apache 2.0.

## Tools that manage master data

Sales has one customer list and finance has another. Master data is the core list of things your business cares about, such as customers and products, and master data management (MDM) keeps one trusted record of each so nobody holds two versions.

### 12. Informatica: for large companies that want many data jobs from one vendor

**At a glance:** Informatica's MDM page describes MDM as the process of creating and maintaining a single, trusted record for customers, products and more. Salesforce completed its acquisition of Informatica on November 18, 2025, per Salesforce's press release.

**What's good**

- Its site lists catalog, integration, quality, MDM and governance products, so it spans several of the five jobs.
- Its [pricing page](https://www.informatica.com/pricing.html) says Informatica Processing Units (IPUs) give access to all eligible cloud services listed in its Cloud and Product Description Schedule.
- The page's menu lists Free Cloud Data Integration.
- The Cloud and Product Description Schedule (version 39.2, effective September 11, 2026) shows Database Ingestion and Replication CDC falling from 6.5 IPU per million rows for the first 15 million to 0.55 up to 750 million and 0.05 above that.

**Where it falls short**

- The pricing page explains IPUs, a usage meter, and ends with a "Get Quote" button. No dollar price per unit appears, and the rates by service live in the separate schedule, so get a quote before you budget.
- Under the IPU model, unused IPUs do not carry over to the next month, and under the Flex IPU model they are consumed over an annual period instead.
- Flex IPU use above your subscription is charged at the overage fee in your order, and the fee is not published.
- IPU and Flex IPU must be provisioned separately and may not be deployed in the same Org.
- The schedule does not say whether a volume tier applies to your whole quantity or only to the units inside each band.

### 13. Semarchy xDM: for teams that want a focused MDM tool

![Semarchy xDM home page showing its headline and main navigation](/img/blog/2026/10/landing-semarchy.webp)

*Semarchy xDM home page, October 2026.*

**At a glance:** Semarchy's platform page says it handles customer, product, supplier, location and reference data, plus any domain you model, in one model.

**What's good**

- Deployment choice: the page lists SaaS, on-premises, cloud and Snowflake.
- It says you can write custom match rules or use vector embeddings for entity resolution with SemQL, its SQL-like language.
- It says lineage is tracked down to the individual record.
- It says generated REST APIs connect ERP, BI, ML models or external targets.
- The page offers a free trial.

**Where it falls short**

- No price is published, so you'll have to contact the company for a quote.

## Where all-in-one suites fit

Fivetran's guide on its site argues that unified tools reduce the work of running many point tools. Keep in mind that's a vendor view. The same page calls its own list "specialized data management tools you can assemble alongside others."

Choose a suite if you already have one vendor, a budget for a contract and few engineers to wire tools together. Choose separate tools if you want open source, a lower starting cost or the freedom to swap one part later.

## A zero-licence starting stack (and what it still costs)

Here's one way to start with no licence fees, using only prices shown above. Run Airbyte Core yourself, query in BigQuery under its free 1 TiB of queries a month, catalog with OpenMetadata, and check rules with Great Expectations. The software price is $0 for each of those pieces on the free terms listed above.

But "no licence" isn't "free." You still pay for servers, for BigQuery storage and any queries past the free tier, and for the engineers who keep four tools working. A paid starting point is Airbyte Cloud Standard at $20 a month with 5 credits, which swaps the server work for a bill.

## How to choose a data management platform

1. **Name the pain.** Data people can't reach needs a pipeline and a store. Data they don't trust needs a catalog, quality checks or master data.
2. **Check the meter.** Rows, credits, scanned bytes, governed assets and processing units all grow differently, and that's where bills surprise people.
3. **Count who will run it.** Open-source tools are free to license, but they still need servers and people.
4. **Ask for the missing price in writing.** Collibra, Semarchy and Informatica each want you to ask for a quote.
5. **Test one tool before adding a second.** Add jobs once the first one works.

Snowflake, BigQuery and Databricks all document support for Apache Iceberg, an open table format for data kept in your own storage (see the Iceberg docs of Snowflake, BigQuery and Databricks). If you keep data there, the copy step matters a lot, and OLake Go, covered in the first group above, is built for it.

## The short version, again

A data management platform isn't one product you buy once. It's a set of jobs: move the data, store it, find it, check it and agree on one master record. Match each job to a tool, and add them in the order your problems show up.

The cheapest way to start is usually a free tier or an open-source tool for the first job. And if a vendor doesn't publish a price, ask for a written quote before you commit.
